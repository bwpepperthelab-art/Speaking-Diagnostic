Speak Your Way Out · Day 1

Speaking Diagnostic

Four short speaking tasks. The computer listens, counts and compares, and you listen back to yourself. At the end you'll know what to train with the speaking game you build.

Work aloneNo AI toolsOne take per taskNo going backUse Chrome on a laptopUse headphones with a mic if you can

Check your microphone

Press the button and say: “Hello, my name is … and I'm ready.” Chrome will ask to use your microphone. Choose Allow.

How it works

  • 1 Read aloudRead a short story. The computer shows which words it couldn't understand.
  • 2 One minuteTalk about a topic for 60 seconds. Then listen back and count your “um”s and long pauses.
  • 3 Role-playAnswer five people in five real situations, from a hotel complaint to disagreeing with a grade.
  • 4 PictureChoose one of three busy photos and describe it for up to 2½ minutes.
  • My resultsRate yourself, see what the computer measured, reflect, make your game plan, and download your results for e-campus.

One way only. When you move to the next step, the step before it closes. You can't go back to redo a task or change an answer, so finish each step before you press Next.

Your work saves in this browser as you go. Don't clear your browser history until you've uploaded your results.

Task 1 · Pronunciation

Read aloud

Read the story once silently. When you're ready, press Start speaking and read it aloud at a natural speed. Press Stop when you finish.

Task 2 · Fluency

One-minute talk

Choose one topic. You get 30 seconds to think (no writing), then talk for one full minute. Keep going until the timer stops you.

Task 3 · Interaction

Role-play replies

Press Hear it, then answer the person out loud. You have 45 seconds for each reply. Some situations need careful, diplomatic language, so think for a moment before you press Start. Speak to them as you would in real life.

Task 4 · Vocabulary

Describe the picture

Choose one of the three photos and open it. Imagine you are describing it to a friend on the phone who can't see it. Say where things are and what people are doing. Look at it for a moment first, then press Start speaking. You have up to 2½ minutes.

Finish

My results

    Step 1 · Rate yourself

    Before you see the computer's results, choose the sentence that describes you best for each skill. The sentences come from the CEFR levels A2, B1, B2 and C1. Be honest: nobody gets a grade for this.

    Step 2 · What the computer measured

    For each skill the computer estimates a CEFR level (A2, B1, B2 or C1) from what it measured in the task. Below each estimate you can see what was measured, the rule for each level, and where the rule comes from. The level you chose sits next to it.

    The computer only knows what speech recognition heard, and it can miss words even when a person would understand you. Treat these levels as estimates from one short task, not grades.

    Step 3 · Reflection

    Write in English, in your own words. Short, honest answers are fine.

    Step 4 · Game plan

    Turn what you learned into a plan for the speaking game you will build.

    Step 5 · Submit on e-campus

    Download your results, then upload the PDF to the e-campus assignment for today.
